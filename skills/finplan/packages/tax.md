## Tools

All dollar inputs are cents; rates are decimals (0.22 = 22%). Only the tools with something the schema does not say are listed here; the rest are covered by the tool index.

### calculate_state_and_local_income_tax

Covers all 50 states + DC (resident returns, before state credits) plus the local layers in the index. Returns `success: false` with a message identifying the unsupported field when a state/locality/year combination has no implementation.

Income goes in as a **breakdown by kind**, not a single taxable-income figure, because states tax kinds of income differently — NY excludes Social Security in full and up to $20,000 of pension/annuity income for a filer who has reached 59.5, and the breakdown reaches the local calculation too. All component amounts are stated **after** any state-level deduction you apply: the tool applies **no standard or itemized deduction of its own** (WI and CT excepted), and `state_standard_deduction_cents` is reported for reference only. For NY, pass `ny_itemized_deductions` to have the tool apply the deduction instead — see below. Pass `age` whenever the filer is retired: the retirement exclusions are age-gated (NY's requires 59.5), and omitting it forfeits them.

**On a joint return, split the retirement income per spouse.** The exclusion belongs to each spouse and applies to _their own_ pension — an unused spouse's cap is not transferable. So pass `spouse_age` **and** put the spouse's retirement income in `spouse_pension_cents` / `spouse_retirement_distribution_cents` rather than pooling it into `pension_cents`. A NY couple both 59.5+, each with a pension, excludes $40,000; pool both pensions into `pension_cents` and they get only $20,000 (which overstates their tax — the split is what claims the second exclusion). `pension_cents` and `retirement_distribution_cents` are the taxpayer's own on a joint return, and `spouse_age` is ignored unless `filing_status` is `married_joint`.

Other input notes:

- `other_income_cents` is ordinary income with no state exclusion attached.
- `federal_agi_cents` is for states whose exclusions carry an income cutoff, `federal_taxable_income_cents` for states that start from it, and `federal_tax_paid_cents` for the states that subtract it (OR, AL, MO). `0` means not supplied.
- `long_term_capital_gains_cents` is the long-term part of `capital_gains_cents`, for Washington's capital gains excise tax only. It is not added to total income. State it before WA's standard deduction and after WA's exemptions (real estate, retirement-account assets).
- With `ny_itemized_deductions`, state income **before** any deduction; the tool applies the larger of NY itemized or standard. `{}` applies the standard deduction alone.

The two effective-rate pairs differ whenever income is excluded: `state_effective_rate` / `combined_effective_rate` are over the state **taxable base** (`state_taxable_income_cents`, matching `calculate_federal_income_tax`), while the `_on_total_income` variants are over the total reported income — the lower figure, and the one a filer usually means by "my effective rate."

**Washington's capital gains tax is its own line.** WA levies no income tax, but it taxes long-term gains above a standard deduction at 7% (9.9% above $1,000,000). It is an excise, not an income tax, so `state_capital_gains_excise_tax_cents` stays out of `state_tax_cents`, `total_state_and_local_tax_cents`, and every rate; `total_tax_including_capital_gains_excise_cents` is the all-in figure. The tax is owed only on `long_term_capital_gains_cents` — passing `capital_gains_cents` alone owes none, so a WA filer selling appreciated long-term assets must state the long-term part. When it is missing and the gains would owe the tax if all long-term, `explanation` says so and prices that case. Every other state returns `0` on that line.

**529 contributions: the state benefit is its own block.** Pass `plan_529_contributions` (each `{plan_state, amount_cents, beneficiary_id?, contributor?}`) to answer "what does this 529 contribution save me in state tax?" `plan_529_benefit` gives the `kind` applied (`deduction`, `credit`, or `none`), `deduction_cents`, `credit_cents`, `contributions_over_cap_cents`, and the state, local and total tax saved, measured after state credits with and without the contributions. Its `notes` say when the state has no 529 record or counts only its own plan. A deduction also lowers `state_taxable_income_cents` and `state_tax_cents`, and NYC's and the Maryland counties' local tax. A credit (Indiana's, or Minnesota's when it beats the subtraction) is **not** in `state_tax_cents`, which is before state credits. Minnesota's credit phases out by income, so pass `federal_agi_cents` with it; without it the credit is lost. Few states carry a 529 record yet; a state without one reports `none` and says so in `notes`, which understates the benefit where the state has one. This is a comparison of tax attributes, not a view on which plan to choose.

**NY deduction: pass the itemized amounts, not a hand-computed deduction.** NY itemizes under its own rules — the pre-2018 federal ones: no SALT cap on property tax, state and local income taxes not deductible, a higher mortgage-debt cap — and then limits itemized deductions at higher incomes twice: an overall cut read at federal AGI, and a NY-AGI reduction that steps up with income until, at the top, only a share of charitable gifts survives. Both are easy to miss by hand. With `ny_itemized_deductions`, `ny_deduction` reports `deduction_type` (`itemized` or `standard`), `deduction_cents`, `itemized_deduction_cents`, `standard_deduction_cents`, each step (`itemized_total_cents`, `mortgage_interest_disallowed_cents`, `federal_agi_limitation_cents`, `income_tax_subtraction_cents`, `itemized_before_high_income_adjustment_cents`, `high_income_adjustment_cents`), `limitations_disallowed_cents` (what the income-based limits took in all), and `notes`. Pass `federal_agi_cents` too — without it the federal-AGI limit is read at NY AGI, and `notes` says so. The deduction also flows into NYC's tax.

### calculate_federal_tax_liability

Composes the underlying primitives. When the filer itemizes, the deductible SALT amount is automatically added back as an AMT exclusion preference. Input notes:

- `wages_cents` is regular W-2 wages and must exclude RSU/NQSO supplemental compensation (use the `supplemental_ordinary_*` fields). It must also exclude any `foreign_earned_income_exclusion_cents` (IRC §911), which is added back to reach MAGI for the NIIT and the SALT phase-down.
- `qualified_dividends_cents` is a subset of `ordinary_dividends_cents` (1099-DIV Box 1a).
- `above_the_line_adjustments_cents` covers 401(k), HSA, SE tax/2, etc.
- `mortgage_origination_year` is required when `mortgage_interest_cents > 0` (ignored otherwise): pre-2018 gets the $1M grandfathered cap, 2018+ gets $750K.
- Charitable cash and appreciated-asset gifts are subject to IRC §170 AGI ceilings (a lower one for appreciated long-term capital-gain property).
- `medical_expenses_cents`: only the portion above 7.5% of AGI is deductible (IRC §213(a)).
- `casualty_theft_loss_cents` is from a single federally declared disaster event and is deductible only when `federally_declared_disaster` is `true` (default `false`).
- `iso_preference_cents` is the ISO exercise-and-hold bargain element (AMT deferral preference).
- `tax_year` is 2026 only.

### calculate_amt

Preference items split into deferral items (e.g. the ISO exercise-and-hold spread), which generate a recoverable Form 8801 credit, and exclusion items (e.g. the SALT add-back), which do not. A standard-deduction filer passes the standard deduction in `other_exclusion_preferences_cents`: line 2a adds it back in place of SALT.

### model_iso_exercise

`intent` is `"hold"` (exercise-and-hold, AMT preference) or `"disqualify_same_year"` (same-year sale, ordinary income, FICA-exempt). `ytd_supplemental_paid_cents` is unused for ISO and accepted for symmetry. The result carries `shares_surrendered_for_strike`, `shares_surrendered_for_tax`, and `shares_retained` as share-surrender placeholders for the cashless / net-exercise scenario. `tax_year` is 2026 only.

### model_nqso_exercise

The bargain element is (FMV − strike) × shares. Supplemental-wage withholding is a flat 22% up to the $1M aggregate YTD cap and a mandatory 37% above; `ytd_supplemental_paid_cents` positions the spread against that cap. `tax_year` is 2026 only.

### model_rsu_vest

Same withholding rule as `model_nqso_exercise` (flat 22% up to the $1M aggregate YTD cap, mandatory 37% above); `ytd_supplemental_paid_cents` positions the vest value against the cap. `tax_year` is 2026 only.

### calculate_payroll_tax

Payroll tax is per person — call once per earner, never with a household's combined wages. The person's wages use the Social Security wage base first; SECA's 12.4% Social Security portion applies only to the room left, on 92.35% of net profit (IRC §1402(a)(12)), and is zero under $400 of net earnings (IRC §1402(b)(2)). Pass `deductible_half_of_self_employment_tax_cents` (summed across earners) as `above_the_line_adjustments_cents` to `calculate_federal_tax_liability` or `analyze_roth_conversion`. The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax is **not** included; `calculate_federal_tax_liability` reports it, so the two results add without double-counting. To cover self-employment earnings, pass `net_earnings_from_self_employment_cents` (summed across earners) to `calculate_federal_tax_liability`, which applies Form 8959 Part II: the threshold is reduced by wages first.

### get_tax_parameters

Each dollar scalar is returned in both `_cents` and `_dollars` form, plus a `source` citation string. `tax_year` is 2026 only. Pass `age` (age at the end of `tax_year`) to also get the person's limits for that age:

- `retirement_contribution_limit`: `catchup_tier` is `none` under 50, `standard` at 50-59 and 64+, `super` at 60-63.
- `ira_contribution_limit_for_age`: tier `none` under 50, `standard` from 50. The IRA limit is shared by traditional and Roth contributions and does not apply the Roth income phase-out.
- `simple_ira_limit_for_age`: tiers as for a 401(k), plus the same three fields as `small_employer_*` for the higher limit an employer with 25 or fewer employees (or 26-100 that elects it) gets; that plan's age-50 catch-up can be the smaller one.
- `hsa_limit_for_age`: tier `none` under 55, `standard` from 55.

A SEP-IRA contribution is the lesser of `sep_contribution_rate` of compensation (counted up to `compensation_limit`) and `total_annual_additions_limit`, with no catch-up.

### apply_after_tax_to_projection_result

Takes a `run_projection` result and computes `after_tax_percentiles` from account-specific withdrawal tax rules. The working chain is in [After-tax projections](projection.md#after-tax-projections). Pass `projection_ref` (preferred; the server loads the full projection, percentiles included) or an inline `projection_result_json`, never both; an inline result must contain a non-empty `percentiles` dict. `marginal_ordinary_rate` and `ltcg_rate` are decimals (0.22 = 22%). For a taxable brokerage account, `initial_cost_basis_cents` is the original invested amount; cost basis is fixed and the gain fraction grows.

**Key behaviors by account type:**

- **pre_tax** (Traditional 401k/IRA): spendable = balance × (1 − marginal_rate)
- **post_tax_deferred** (Roth): spendable = balance (identity, no tax)
- **tax_advantaged** (HSA, 529): spendable = balance (identity for qualified expenses)
- **taxable** cash (savings, checking): spendable = balance (no withdrawal tax)
- **taxable** brokerage: spendable = balance − (gains × ltcg_rate)

The full adjusted ProjectionResult, with monthly `after_tax_percentiles`, is at `urls.data`. A projection with no percentiles is an error, not an empty result.

### analyze_roth_conversion

The marginal federal tax cost is two full-liability calls diffed, so bracket-stacking, NIIT, and AMT are captured. The conversion amount is given either explicitly (`conversion_amount_cents`), by filling a target ordinary bracket (`fill_to_marginal_rate`, e.g. `0.24`), or optimized (`recommend`, "how much should I convert?") — provide exactly one. Recommend mode returns the largest amount whose next-dollar marginal cost rate stays at or below `future_marginal_rate` (0 when converting nothing is best); it is federal-only and cliff-unaware (does not stop short of an IRMAA/ACA surcharge edge). The break-even assumes the conversion tax is paid from the converted (IRA) funds. Out of scope: IRMAA tiers, ACA subsidy cliffs, multi-year conversion ladders.

Input notes:

- `future_marginal_rate` is a decimal (0.30 = 30%); `expected_annual_return` defaults to `0.06`.
- The federal income components follow the same rules as `calculate_federal_tax_liability`: `wages_cents` must exclude RSU/NQSO supplemental compensation, and `mortgage_origination_year` is required when `mortgage_interest_cents > 0` (ignored otherwise).
- Provide `state_code` (with the `state_*` income components) to fold resident state+local income tax on the conversion into the cost and break-even; unsupported states return `success: false`. `locality` is ignored when `state_code` is omitted. Every `state_*` component defaults to 0, and `state_code` with all of them left at 0 is accepted as a (degenerate) all-zero state base. State components are stated after any state deduction the caller applies.
- `state_retirement_distribution_cents` is distributions already received this year, before the conversion. The conversion amount is added automatically as an additional taxpayer retirement distribution — it is not entered here. The conversion is attributed to the taxpayer, not the spouse, so `state_spouse_retirement_distribution_cents` excludes it too. On a joint return, `state_pension_cents` is the taxpayer's; the spouse's goes in `state_spouse_pension_cents`.
- `state_age` gates age-gated state retirement exclusions (e.g. NY's pension exclusion requires 59.5), and also gates the exclusion for the converted amount itself, since it is classified as a retirement distribution. Leaving it at 0 forfeits the exclusion. `state_spouse_age` is ignored unless `filing_status` is `married_joint`.
- `state_federal_agi_cents`, `state_federal_taxable_income_cents`, and `state_federal_tax_paid_cents` (for the states that subtract it: OR, AL, MO) mean not supplied at 0.
- `future_state_marginal_rate` is a decimal (e.g. `0.0685`). When `state_code` is set but this is omitted, the conversion's own state+local marginal cost rate is used as the proxy, so state is then a wash in the decision.
- `early_withdrawal_penalty_cents` is the IRC §72(t) penalty on the IRA funds used to pay the conversion tax when the owner is under 59½ (`0` otherwise), folded into `break_even_future_rate`. Its base is the tax actually paid from the IRA: federal-only, or federal+state when `state_code` is set. The penalty is itself paid from the IRA and so penalizes itself, making the effective rate charged on that tax `0.10/0.90` ≈ 11.11%, not 10%.
- When `state_code` is set, the result also carries the `state_*` and `combined_*` cost fields, and `break_even` reflects the combined cost plus any penalty.

## Usage notes

- All income in **cents**. 10000000 = $100,000.
- Filing status uses short strings: `"married_joint"` not `"married_filing_jointly"`.
- For after-tax projections: first run `run_projection`, then pass its `projection_ref` to `apply_after_tax_to_projection_result`.
