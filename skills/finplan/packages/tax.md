## Tools

### calculate_federal_income_tax

Calculate federal income tax for given income and filing status. Includes marginal rate, effective rate, LTCG rate, and explanation. Parameters are in the tool index above; `filing_status` is one of `"single"`, `"married_joint"`, `"married_separate"`, `"head_of_household"`, `"qualifying_widow"`, and `tax_year` is a tax year (default: `2026`).

Returns: `tax_owed_cents`, `tax_owed_dollars`, `marginal_rate`, `effective_rate`, `marginal_rate_percent`, `effective_rate_percent`, `ltcg_rate`, `ltcg_rate_percent`, `explanation`.

### calculate_capital_gains_tax_rate

Get the long-term capital gains tax rate for an income level. Parameters are in the tool index; `filing_status` takes the options above, and `tax_year` is a tax year (default: `2026`).

Returns: `ltcg_rate` (float, e.g., 0.15 = 15%).

### calculate_state_and_local_income_tax

Calculate resident state and (optionally) local income tax using full progressive brackets. **Covers all 50 states + DC** (resident returns, before state credits), plus the local layers: NYC, Yonkers, every Maryland county, Philadelphia, Detroit, Columbus/Cleveland/Cincinnati/Toledo, St. Louis, Kansas City, Louisville, Birmingham, and Wilmington. Returns `success: false` with a message identifying the unsupported field when a state/locality/year combination has no implementation.

Income goes in as a **breakdown by kind**, not a single taxable-income figure, because states tax kinds of income differently — NY excludes Social Security in full and up to $20,000 of pension/annuity income for a filer who has reached 59.5, and the breakdown reaches the local calculation too. All component amounts are stated **after** any state-level deduction you apply: the tool applies **no standard or itemized deduction of its own** (WI and CT excepted), and `state_standard_deduction_cents` is reported for reference only. For NY, pass `ny_itemized_deductions` to have the tool apply the deduction instead — see below. Pass `age` whenever the filer is retired: the retirement exclusions are age-gated, and omitting it forfeits them.

**On a joint return, split the retirement income per spouse.** The exclusion belongs to each spouse and applies to _their own_ pension — an unused spouse's cap is not transferable. So pass `spouse_age` **and** put the spouse's retirement income in `spouse_pension_cents` / `spouse_retirement_distribution_cents` rather than pooling it into `pension_cents`. A NY couple both 59.5+, each with a pension, excludes $40,000; pool both pensions into `pension_cents` and they get only $20,000 (which overstates their tax — the split is what claims the second exclusion).

The two effective-rate pairs differ whenever income is excluded: `state_effective_rate` / `combined_effective_rate` are over the state **taxable base** (matching `calculate_federal_income_tax`), while the `_on_total_income` variants are over the total reported income — the lower figure, and the one a filer usually means by "my effective rate."

**Washington's capital gains tax is its own line.** WA levies no income tax, but it taxes long-term gains above a standard deduction at 7% (9.9% above $1,000,000). It is an excise, not an income tax, so `state_capital_gains_excise_tax_cents` stays out of `state_tax_cents`, `total_state_and_local_tax_cents`, and every rate; `total_tax_including_capital_gains_excise_cents` is the all-in figure. The tax is owed only on `long_term_capital_gains_cents` — passing `capital_gains_cents` alone owes none, so a WA filer selling appreciated long-term assets must state the long-term part. When it is missing and the gains would owe the tax if all long-term, `explanation` says so and prices that case. Every other state returns `0` on that line.

**529 contributions: the state benefit is its own block.** Pass `plan_529_contributions` to answer "what does this 529 contribution save me in state tax?" `plan_529_benefit` gives the `kind` applied (`deduction`, `credit`, or `none`), `deduction_cents`, `credit_cents`, `contributions_over_cap_cents`, and the state, local and total tax saved, measured after state credits with and without the contributions. Its `notes` say when the state has no 529 record or counts only its own plan. A deduction also lowers `state_taxable_income_cents` and `state_tax_cents`, and NYC's and the Maryland counties' local tax. A credit (Indiana's, or Minnesota's when it beats the subtraction) is **not** in `state_tax_cents`, which is before state credits. Minnesota's credit phases out by income, so pass `federal_agi_cents` with it; without it the credit is lost. Few states carry a 529 record yet; a state without one reports `none` and says so in `notes`, which understates the benefit where the state has one. This is a comparison of tax attributes, not a view on which plan to choose.

**NY deduction: pass the itemized amounts, not a hand-computed deduction.** NY itemizes under its own rules — the pre-2018 federal ones: no SALT cap on property tax, state and local income taxes not deductible, a higher mortgage-debt cap — and then limits itemized deductions at higher incomes twice: an overall cut read at federal AGI, and a NY-AGI reduction that steps up with income until, at the top, only a share of charitable gifts survives. Both are easy to miss by hand. With `ny_itemized_deductions`, `ny_deduction` reports `deduction_type` (`itemized` or `standard`), `deduction_cents`, `itemized_deduction_cents`, `standard_deduction_cents`, each step (`itemized_total_cents`, `mortgage_interest_disallowed_cents`, `federal_agi_limitation_cents`, `income_tax_subtraction_cents`, `itemized_before_high_income_adjustment_cents`, `high_income_adjustment_cents`), `limitations_disallowed_cents` (what the income-based limits took in all), and `notes`. Pass `federal_agi_cents` too — without it the federal-AGI limit is read at NY AGI, and `notes` says so. The deduction also flows into NYC's tax.

### calculate_payroll_tax

Calculate one person's payroll tax for a year: employee FICA (6.2% Social Security on wages up to the wage base, 1.45% Medicare) and self-employment tax (SECA) on net self-employment profit. Payroll tax is per person — call once per earner, never with a household's combined wages. The person's wages use the Social Security wage base first; SECA's 12.4% Social Security portion applies only to the room left, on 92.35% of net profit (IRC §1402(a)(12)), and is zero under $400 of net earnings (IRC §1402(b)(2)). Pass `deductible_half_of_self_employment_tax_cents` (summed across earners) as `above_the_line_adjustments_cents` to `calculate_federal_tax_liability` or `analyze_roth_conversion`. The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax is **not** included. `calculate_federal_tax_liability` reports it on W-2 wages and FICA-subject supplemental pay only, so the two results add without double-counting; on self-employment earnings neither tool reports it, so take-home for SE income above the threshold is overstated by up to 0.9% of the excess.

### apply_after_tax_to_projection_result

Apply withdrawal taxes to a projection result, computing after-tax spendable values by account type. Takes a `run_projection` result, by `projection_ref`, and computes `after_tax_percentiles` from account-specific withdrawal tax rules. The working chain is in [After-tax projections](projection.md#after-tax-projections).

| Parameter                  | Type   | Description                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------- | ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `projection_ref`           | string | `projection_ref` from `run_projection`. The server loads the full projection, percentiles included. Preferred.                          |
| `projection_result_json`   | dict   | A complete ProjectionResult passed inline. Must contain a non-empty `percentiles` dict. Use instead of `projection_ref`, never with it. |
| `account_tax_treatment`    | string | `"pre_tax"`, `"post_tax_deferred"`, `"taxable"`, or `"tax_advantaged"`                                                                  |
| `marginal_ordinary_rate`   | float  | Marginal ordinary income tax rate (0.22 = 22%)                                                                                          |
| `ltcg_rate`                | float  | Long-term capital gains tax rate (0.15 = 15%)                                                                                           |
| `taxable_income_type`      | string | `"none"` (default), `"ordinary_income"`, or `"investment_income"`                                                                       |
| `initial_cost_basis_cents` | int    | For taxable brokerage: original invested amount in cents. Cost basis is fixed; gain fraction grows. (optional)                          |

**Key behaviors by account type:**

- **pre_tax** (Traditional 401k/IRA): spendable = balance × (1 − marginal_rate)
- **post_tax_deferred** (Roth): spendable = balance (identity, no tax)
- **tax_advantaged** (HSA, 529): spendable = balance (identity for qualified expenses)
- **taxable** cash (savings, checking): spendable = balance (no withdrawal tax)
- **taxable** brokerage: spendable = balance − (gains × ltcg_rate)

Returns: `final_balance_percentiles` (final-month `pre_tax_cents` / `after_tax_cents` per percentile), `summary`, and `urls` (the full adjusted ProjectionResult, with monthly `after_tax_percentiles`, at `urls.data`). A projection with no percentiles is an error, not an empty result.

### analyze_roth_conversion

The conversion amount is given either explicitly (`conversion_amount_cents`), by filling a target ordinary bracket (`fill_to_marginal_rate`), or optimized (`recommend`, "how much should I convert?") — provide exactly one. Recommend mode returns the largest amount whose next-dollar marginal cost rate stays at or below `future_marginal_rate` (0 when converting nothing is best); it is federal-only and cliff-unaware (does not stop short of an IRMAA/ACA surcharge edge). The break-even assumes the conversion tax is paid from the converted (IRA) funds. Provide `state_code` (with the `state_*` income components) to fold resident state+local income tax on the conversion into the cost and break-even; unsupported states return `success: false`. ## Usage notes

- All income in **cents**. 10000000 = $100,000.
- Filing status uses short strings: `"married_joint"` not `"married_filing_jointly"`.
- For after-tax projections: first run `run_projection`, then pass its `projection_ref` to `apply_after_tax_to_projection_result`.
